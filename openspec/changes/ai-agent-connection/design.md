## Context

See `proposal.md` for motivation and ADR 0068 for the choice of Chatwoot's agent bot. Facts that shape the approach, read in Chatwoot v4.19.1 (paths relative to Chatwoot's tree):

- **Agent bots are in the Community Edition**, on by default (`config/features.yml:46-48`), with account routes `agent_bots` and an inbox route `set_agent_bot` (`config/routes.rb:145`, `:340`). A bot is a webhook bot with an `outgoing_url` (`app/models/agent_bot.rb`); its `secret` is generated by Chatwoot and encrypted when encryption is configured (`app/models/concerns/webhook_secretable.rb`); its access token is made at creation and destroyed asynchronously with it (`app/models/concerns/access_tokenable.rb:4-5`). An inbox has at most one bot (`app/models/inbox.rb:76-77`, `inboxes_controller.rb:69-78`), and a Dialogflow integration counts as one (`app/models/concerns/inbox_bot_status.rb:8-10`, `agent_bot_inbox.rb:17`).
- **What the bot receives.** `AgentBotListener` (`app/listeners/agent_bot_listener.rb`) sends the inbox's active bot `message_created` and `message_updated` for every message that is incoming, outgoing or a template (`webhook_sendable?`, `app/models/concerns/message_filter_helpers.rb:8-10`), whatever the conversation's status: the shopper's messages, the bot's own replies, the team's replies **and the team's private notes** (an outgoing message with `private: true`). Activity messages are not sent. It also sends `conversation_opened`, `conversation_resolved`, `conversation_status_changed`, `conversation_updated` and `webwidget_triggered` (lines 2-61).
- **The message payload** (`app/models/message.rb:184-202`): `id`, `content`, `content_type`, `content_attributes`, `message_type`, `private`, `created_at`, `source_id`, `sender` (`type` is `user` or `agent_bot`; a contact's payload has no `type`, only `contact_type`, `app/models/contact.rb:159-173`, seen on the stand for task 3.6), `inbox`, `account`, `conversation`, and `attachments` when there are any. A conversation in the payload (`app/presenters/conversations/event_data_presenter.rb:2-50`) carries `id` (the display id the API takes), `inbox_id`, `status`, `priority`, `labels`, `custom_attributes`, `meta.sender` (the contact), `meta.assignee`, `meta.assignee_type`, `meta.team`, and only its last message. An attachment (`app/models/attachment.rb:50-54`, `:124-135`) carries `id`, `message_id`, `file_type` (`image`, `audio`, `video`, `file`, …), `extension`, `content_type`, `file_size`, `width`, `height`, `data_url` (a redirect to the file) and `thumb_url`.
- **Files from the widget.** The widget sends each file as a message of its own, with no text (`app/javascript/widget/api/endPoints.js:46-62`, `components/ChatFooter.vue:83-89`); the attachment is built on the message before it is saved (`api/v1/widget/messages_controller.rb:10-13`, `:33-42`), so `message_created` already carries it. Two photos are two messages with one attachment each. The upload limit is `MAXIMUM_FILE_UPLOAD_SIZE`, 40 MB by default (`app/models/attachment.rb:208-212`).
- **Signing and delivery** (`lib/webhooks/trigger.rb`). The body is JSON; with a secret, `X-Chatwoot-Timestamp` is the Unix time and `X-Chatwoot-Signature` is `sha256=` + hex HMAC-SHA256 of `"{timestamp}.{body}"`; `X-Chatwoot-Delivery` is a UUID per event, kept across retries (lines 54-63; `app/listeners/agent_bot_listener.rb:87-88`). The read and open timeouts are `WEBHOOK_TIMEOUT`, 5 seconds by default (lines 118-123). Only HTTP 429 and 500 are retried, three attempts three seconds apart (line 3, lines 125-127; `app/jobs/agent_bots/webhook_job.rb:3`). Any other failure (another status, a timeout, a refused connection) ends the delivery; for a message event in a **pending** conversation it moves the conversation to Open with Chatwoot's activity message, unless the account setting `keep_pending_on_bot_failure` is on (lines 65-89).
- **Outbound requests pass an SSRF filter**: private and internal addresses are refused unless `SAFE_FETCH_ALLOW_PRIVATE_NETWORK` is set (`lib/safe_fetch.rb:36-37`, `lib/safe_fetch/fetcher.rb:47-49`).
- **Pending and handover.** A new conversation in an inbox with an active bot starts as `pending`, with the bot as its AI assignee (`app/models/conversation.rb:316-333`; Chatwoot's own comment at :329 calls this an assumption to make configurable). A shopper's message in a resolved conversation of such an inbox makes it pending again (`app/models/message.rb:435-444`). The bot hands over with `toggle_status` `open` on a pending conversation: `bot_handoff!` clears the AI assignee, sets `waiting_since` and opens it (`conversations_controller.rb:83-100`, `conversation.rb:184-193`). A person opening it through the dashboard takes it and is assigned (`conversations_controller.rb:93`, `:181-187`). Auto-assignment runs when a conversation becomes Open and never while a bot is its assignee (`app/models/concerns/auto_assignment_handler.rb:24-28`, `:58-66`). **A person's public reply does not open a pending conversation in the Community Edition**: the hook that would (`mark_pending_conversation_as_open_for_human_response`) returns at once because `captain_pending_conversation?` is false (`message.rb:423-433`).
- **The dashboard keeps a pending conversation of the bot to private notes.** While a conversation is pending with an AI assignee, the reply box offers only "Private Note", under a banner "This conversation is currently handled by …" with "Take over" (`app/javascript/dashboard/components/widgets/conversation/ReplyBoxBanner.vue`). "Take over" assigns the person through the assignments API and marks the conversation open only in the browser's store; in the Community Edition the server leaves it pending, so D5's assignment rule is what opens it. A public reply without taking over comes from other clients of the API. Seen on the stand (task 3.6).
- **What a bot token may call** (`app/controllers/concerns/access_token_auth_helper.rb:2-7`): conversations `show`, `toggle_status`, `toggle_typing_status`, `toggle_priority`, `create`, `update`, `custom_attributes`; messages `create`; assignments `create`; labels `index` and `create`. Not the message list, contacts, inboxes or account labels. A bot can enter an account only through an inbox it is attached to (`ensure_current_account_helper.rb:29-31`), but once in, Chatwoot's policy lets it act on **any** conversation of that account (`app/policies/conversation_policy.rb:11`, `:24-25`). The token is sent as `api_access_token` or as `Authorization: Bearer` (`access_token_auth_helper.rb:18-27`).
- **Labels.** `POST conversations/:id/labels` replaces the conversation's labels with the given list (`app/controllers/concerns/label_concern.rb:2-5`, `app/models/concerns/labelable.rb:9-11`). A conversation may carry any tag, but the dashboard shows only those that exist as the account's labels (`app/javascript/dashboard/composables/useConversationLabels.js:44-48`, `components-next/Conversation/ConversationCard/CardLabels.vue:30`). A label title is lower case, letters, digits, `_` and `-` (`app/models/label.rb:25-34`, `lib/regex_helper.rb:9`).
- Priority is `low`, `medium`, `high` or `urgent` (`conversation.rb:87`). The API is throttled per IP, 3000 requests a minute by default, when Rack::Attack is on (`config/initializers/rack_attack.rb:128`).
- The overlay: tables prefixed `inclamo_`, own migration record, encrypted columns refusing to save without keys (`custom/app/models/inclamo/shopify_connection.rb`), listeners added through `Custom::AsyncDispatcher` (`custom/app/dispatchers/custom/async_dispatcher.rb`), settings pages through seam 0002 (`patches/0002-settings-pages.patch`, `shims/app/javascript/dashboard/inclamo/settingsPages.js`).

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- The AI service can be built against `docs/ai-connector.md` alone, in parallel with this change.
- Nothing of the agent acts in an account that is not connected, and nothing is left behind by a refused connection (0063).
- The team can tell at a glance which conversations the agent has (Pending) and which are theirs (Open), and why one came to them.
- Every scenario runs end to end on the stand without the AI service.

**Non-Goals:**
- Subscription, usage and billing; the AI service decides whether it serves an account.
- The agent in email inboxes is allowed by the picker but its behaviour there is the AI service's; only the widget is tested in this change.
- Reading the content of files: the helpdesk delivers file metadata; what the AI service does with files is its own rule.
- Per-inbox control of the "pending first" rule (it would be a core seam).

## Decisions

**D1. One connection per account, in our table.** `inclamo_ai_connections`: `account_id` (unique), `service_url`, `connection_id` (the AI service's), `agent_bot_id`, `phone_inbox_id`, `inbox_ids`, `state` (`connected`, `failing`, `disconnected`), `last_delivered_at`, `last_failed_at`, `last_error`, `last_reply_at`, `connected_by`, timestamps. The bot's token and secret stay in Chatwoot's own tables, so no secret of ours is stored; connecting is refused without encryption keys, as for the store connection, because the bot's secret would otherwise be stored in plain text. A disconnected record is kept for its history and replaced by the next connection.

**D2. The connection code.** The AI service issues a code of the form `aic1.<base64url of JSON>` with `service_url` (HTTPS) and `token` (one-time, short-lived; its lifetime is the AI service's). The helpdesk decodes it and refuses, before any request, a code that does not decode or a URL that is not HTTPS (HTTP is accepted only when `SAFE_FETCH_ALLOW_PRIVATE_NETWORK` is on, as in the test stack). Alternative rejected: the administrator copies the bot's token into the AI service by hand. It is one more secret travelling through a person and leaves the AI service without the inbox ids.

**D3. Connecting, in an order that leaves nothing on refusal.**
1. Create the agent bot named after the agent, with no `outgoing_url`: Chatwoot sends nothing to a bot without one (`agent_bot_listener.rb:85`).
2. Create the "Phone" API inbox, unless the account has ours already from an earlier connection.
3. `POST {service_url}/connections`, `Authorization: Bearer {token}`, body `{ contract: 1, helpdesk_url, account: { id, name }, bot: { id, access_token, secret }, phone_inbox_id, inbox_ids }`. Expected answer `201 { connection_id, webhook_url }`; a `4xx` with `{ error, message }` is a refusal shown to the administrator as the message; anything else, or no answer within 10 s, is "the AI service did not answer". Requests go through SafeFetch.
4. On refusal: destroy the bot and a "Phone" inbox made in step 2. Nothing else was made.
5. On success: set the bot's `outgoing_url` to `webhook_url`; turn `keep_pending_on_bot_failure` off on the account; create the fixed labels (D7); attach the bot to the chosen inboxes; save the record as `connected`.
`helpdesk_url` is the installation's `FRONTEND_URL`. The shape of step 3 is a proposal to agree with the builder of the AI service (assumption 1).

**D4. Disconnecting.** Move every pending conversation of the connected inboxes to Open with `bot_handoff!` (no event is lost: the team gets them); detach the bot from its inboxes; destroy its access token at once, then the bot (Chatwoot destroys the token asynchronously, which would leave it usable for a moment); call `DELETE {service_url}/connections/{connection_id}` with the bot's former token as `Authorization`, best effort, a failure noted on the page; mark the record `disconnected`. "Phone", its conversations and the labels stay.

**D5. A person's reply or assignment opens a pending conversation.** A listener on `message_created` and `assignee_changed` (Chatwoot's `assignee.changed` event, `lib/events/types.rb:33`, dispatched from `app/models/concerns/assignment_handler.rb:33`): in a pending conversation of a connected inbox, a public outgoing message whose sender is a user, or a user becoming the assignee, calls `bot_handoff!`. The agent then receives `conversation_status_changed` and stops. Alternative rejected: leaving it to the AI service to treat a teammate's message as a handover. The rule then lives outside the helpdesk, and a team member who assigns themselves without writing would still see the agent answer.

**D6. Two endpoints for the agent**, under `/api/v1/accounts/:account_id/inclamo/ai/conversations/:conversation_id/` (display id), authenticated by the connected bot's token and refused for a conversation outside the connected inboxes. This is stricter than Chatwoot's own policy for bots.
- `GET messages?limit=30` (at most 100): the conversation's `status`, `inbox_id`, `priority`, `labels`, and its last public messages oldest first, each with `id`, `created_at`, `role` (`shopper` for incoming, `agent` for our bot, `team` for a user), `text` and `files` (`id`, `file_type`, `content_type`, `file_size`, `url`); plus `shopper_files`, the number of files in all the shopper's public messages of the conversation. Private notes and activity messages are left out.
- `POST labels {add: [...]}`: adds the names under a lock on the conversation, keeping every other label, and answers the full list. Only names starting `inclamo_` that match Chatwoot's label format are accepted. A name the account lacks is created as an account label with the agent's colour and the description "Set by the AI agent".
Alternative rejected: adding the message list to the bot's allowed endpoints. It would open every conversation of the account and the team's private notes to the bot. Alternative rejected: Chatwoot's label call, which replaces the team's labels and would race with them.

**D7. Labels.** At connection the helpdesk creates the labels the AI service uses today, with their meanings as descriptions: `inclamo_handover`, `inclamo_resolved`, `inclamo_escalation`, `inclamo_error`, `inclamo_limit`, `inclamo_offline`, `inclamo_urgent`, `inclamo_request_ready`, `inclamo_request_incomplete`, and the call labels `inclamo_call`, `inclamo_inbound`, `inclamo_outbound`, `inclamo_web`, `inclamo_callback`, `inclamo_parts_request`, `inclamo_transferred`, `inclamo_no_conversation`, `inclamo_match_ambiguous`, `inclamo_no_caller_id`. Labels per request type (`inclamo_intent_<id>`) are created by D6 when first added. An existing label of the same title is left as it is.

**D8. Delivery health.** A module prepended from the overlay to `Webhooks::Trigger#perform_request` records, for `agent_bot_webhook` deliveries to a connection's `webhook_url`, success (`last_delivered_at`) or failure (`last_failed_at`, `last_error` as the status or error class, never the body). The state becomes `failing` on a failure and `connected` again on the next success. A reply of our bot (a listener on `message_created`) sets `last_reply_at`. Alternative rejected: reading Chatwoot's activity message "moved to open". It is a localised text, it exists only for pending conversations, and it says nothing about success.

**D9. The page.** `AiAgentPage.vue`, registered in `settingsPages.js` (seam 0002) for administrators. Not connected: the agent's description, a link to get it, the code field, and the inbox picker (every inbox except "Phone"; one with another bot or Dialogflow shown and not selectable). Connected: inboxes, state, the last reply and the last error with their times, and "Disconnect" with a confirmation that says pending conversations will go to the team. The backend endpoints are `GET/POST/DELETE /api/v1/accounts/:account_id/inclamo/ai/connection`, administrators only.

**D10. The stand-in agent.** A small Node service in `e2e/stand-in-agent/`, part of the e2e stack. It issues connection codes, answers the registration call, verifies the signature, refuses timestamps older than five minutes, de-duplicates by `X-Chatwoot-Delivery`, answers `200` at once and then acts through the API by fixed rules on the shopper's text: reply; reply naming the number of files it received (from the event and from `shopper_files`); hand over with a note and `inclamo_handover`; urgent (priority `urgent`, `inclamo_urgent`, hand over); resolve; add `inclamo_intent_test`. A control endpoint lets a test read what it received and make it answer `503`. The e2e stack sets `SAFE_FETCH_ALLOW_PRIVATE_NETWORK` so Chatwoot may reach it; nothing else does. Tests assert what the helpdesk shows, never the agent's words, so the same flows can run against the AI service given a code from it.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Chatwoot's payloads and bot rights are now our contract] → `docs/ai-connector.md` names each field and call by its source line; the upstream-release routine re-runs this change's e2e file.
- [Every conversation in a connected inbox starts Pending; Chatwoot's default list shows Open] → the page and the guide say where the agent's conversations are; per-inbox control would be a core seam, not taken now.
- [A bot token can act on any conversation of the account through Chatwoot's own endpoints] → stated in the contract as a rule for the AI service (act only on conversations of connected inboxes, as delivered to it); our endpoints enforce it; the token never leaves the helpdesk except to the AI service.
- [The webhook timeout is 5 s and a turn takes longer] → the contract requires answering `200` at once and working after.
- [A failure the AI service has after answering `200` leaves the conversation Pending] → the contract requires the AI service to hand over itself (`inclamo_error`); the stand-in tests that path.
- [Prepending a core class from the overlay] → one private method, covered by a spec that fails when Chatwoot renames it.
- [Labels of a removed request type stay in the account] → harmless; the team may delete them.

## Migration Plan

New table only; nothing changes for an account until it connects. Rollback: disconnect, then remove the overlay code; the bot is gone and "Phone" stays as an ordinary API inbox.

## Open Questions

- The connection code's lifetime and how the merchant gets it are the AI service's. The page only links to it.
- The agent's colour for labels. Built with Chatwoot's default label colour, one constant (`Inclamo::Ai::LABEL_COLOR`), until it is chosen.

Found while building (2026-11-07), not settled by the decisions above; each is built the way stated and waits for the founder's word:

- **The bot's name** is "AI agent" (`inclamo.ai_connection.bot_name`). The shopper sees it on the agent's replies in the widget, the team in the conversation. D3 says "named after the agent" without a name.
- **"Get the AI agent" links to `INCLAMO_AI_AGENT_URL`**, `https://inclamo.com` when unset.
- **After a disconnection the agent's earlier replies keep no sender.** Destroying the bot (D4) lets Chatwoot clear the sender of the bot's messages (`app/models/agent_bot.rb`, `has_many :messages, dependent: :nullify`); the replies stay, without "AI agent" on them. The same holds for the agent's turns of filed calls (`call-conversations`).
- **`keep_pending_on_bot_failure` stays off after a disconnection**; D3 turns it off, D4 does not turn it back.
- **A `201` without a `connection_id` or an HTTPS `webhook_url` is "the AI service did not answer"**, and the helpdesk asks, best effort, to remove the `connection_id` it carried; the same when the helpdesk cannot finish after a `201`.
- **Limits of the labels endpoint**: at most 20 names a request, 100 characters a name; more is `422`.
- **`role: team`** also covers outgoing public messages that no person wrote (automation rules, another bot), since only our bot is `agent`.
- **After a failed delivery the conversation is Open but still assigned to the bot.** Chatwoot's failure rule calls `open!`, not `bot_handoff!` (`lib/webhooks/trigger.rb:77-83`), so the AI assignee stays and auto-assignment never gives the conversation to a person; the team finds it under All, not Mine or Unassigned (seen on the stand, `docs/ai-connector.md` §10.3). Clearing the assignee there would be a rule of ours beyond D5.
- **The end-to-end coverage check skips proposed changes that are not built yet** (`PLANNED` in `scripts/check-e2e-coverage.mjs`: `call-conversations`, `hosted-instance`, `shopify-oauth-connection`, `shopify-sign-in`); without it the check, and with it every end-to-end run, fails on this branch. Each change leaves the list when it is built.
